Machine learning & forecasting

Effects of missing-data imputation on ARIMA and LSTM forecasting performance

This study asked how different imputation methods affect downstream forecasting performance in univariate blood-pressure time series. Ten methods were applied first; ARIMA then served as a statistical benchmark for evaluating LSTM predictive performance across four missingness rates (10%–35%). The R and Python workflow used 70 observations, an 80/20 train-test split, walk-forward validation with model re-estimation, and 1,640 model runs.

  • Imputation changed the time-series structure—especially autocorrelation—and the method that best reconstructed missing values did not necessarily produce the best forecasts.
  • Mean imputation deteriorated as missingness increased, while interpolation, Kalman-based, and moving-average methods were more competitive across scenarios.
  • LSTM delivered only a modest overall accuracy improvement; ARIMA remained a fast, interpretable, and competitive benchmark for a small time series.

The underlying study data are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request and are therefore not distributed through GitHub. Read the peer-reviewed study →
