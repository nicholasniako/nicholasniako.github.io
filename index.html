About

I am a Ph.D. student in Biostatistics at the University of South Carolina's Arnold School of Public Health. My research integrates Bayesian statistics, functional data analysis, Markov modeling, machine learning and AI, time series and longitudinal modeling, wearable sensor analytics, and population health to study complex behavioral and health data. I develop quantitative methods and reproducible software for analyzing high-dimensional, temporally structured data from wearable technologies, time-use diaries, and clinical studies. My current work focuses on harmonizing accelerometer-measured and parent-reported behaviors, with applications to physical activity, sleep, sedentary behavior, and population health research.
