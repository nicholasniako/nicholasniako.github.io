About

Nicholas Niako is a Ph.D. student in Biostatistics at the University of South Carolina's Arnold School of Public Health. His research lies at the intersection of statistical methodology, data science, and population health, with interests in missing data analysis, Bayesian methods, functional data analysis, longitudinal modeling, Markov models, time series forecasting, and machine learning.

His current work focuses on wearable sensor data, time-use diaries, and the harmonization of accelerometer-measured behaviors with parent-reported behaviors. More broadly, he develops and applies quantitative methods for analyzing complex, high-dimensional, and temporally structured data arising from clinical studies, wearable sensor technologies, and population health research.

He also develops statistical software and AI and machine-learning methods for wearable-sensor and longitudinal health data, with an emphasis on reproducible tools that connect methodological research to practical population-health applications.
